# Jim White Strategic Pilot Conversation Plan

## Core Strategy

Do not show up as:

**"I have an idea. What do you think?"**

Show up as:

**"I have done field discovery, found a repeat workflow problem, and am organizing a small Bay Area pilot with medium-scale produce/food operators. I want your expert feedback on pilot design and whether this could complement traceability platforms."**

That sounds serious.

## Your Positioning

Use this framing:

```text
I’ve been doing field discovery with Bay Area produce wholesalers, restaurant suppliers, and a larger avocado distributor.

The pattern I’m seeing is that smaller and mid-sized operators often still receive supplier records as paper invoices, handwritten notes, PDFs, labels, Excel, or system exports. Larger operators already have traceability systems, but the operational gap is still around missing or conflicting supplier data.

I’m now planning a small pilot with local medium-scale produce and food operators. The pilot is focused on record cleanup and traceability readiness, not replacing any traceability platform.
```

## What To Say About The Pilot

Say:

```text
The pilot will be intentionally narrow.

I’m planning to work with a few local Bay Area operators. For each operator, I want to take 5 redacted shipment record sets: invoice, BOL, label, receiving record, and ASN if available.

The output will be:
- missing KDE / lot / source field report
- invoice vs BOL vs label mismatch report
- clean Excel or FDA-style export sample
- notes on what would need supplier follow-up

I want to test whether operators recognize this as real work they currently do manually.
```

This makes the pilot sound like an execution plan.

## What Not To Say

Avoid:

- "I don't have an app."
- "I don't know if this is useful."
- "Can you tell me if I should build this?"
- "Can you partner with me?"
- "Can you use my software?"
- "I am just starting and need help."

Those make you sound too early.

Instead, say:

- "I’m starting with a focused pilot."
- "I’m validating the operational workflow before building too much software."
- "The first deliverable is the cleaned output, not a dashboard."
- "I want your input on whether the pilot is structured correctly."

## How To Mention ENSESO4Food

Say:

```text
I looked at ENSESO4Food and it seems like you are solving the traceability platform layer.

What I’m exploring is the messy step before that: when operators do not yet have clean records ready for any traceability system.

I’m trying to understand whether this cleanup step is complementary to platforms like yours.
```

This avoids sounding competitive.

## Strategic Questions To Ask Jim

### 1. Pilot Design

```text
If I run this pilot with local medium-scale produce operators, does 5 redacted shipment record sets per company sound like enough to expose the data-quality problem?
```

```text
What fields should I absolutely check so the pilot is meaningful for FSMA 204 readiness?
```

```text
Would you include invoices, BOLs, labels, receiving records, and ASNs, or am I missing an important document?
```

### 2. Customer Segment

```text
Which segment would feel this pain most: produce distributors, seafood importers, processors, foodservice distributors, or small wholesalers?
```

```text
Do medium-scale operators usually have enough pain to care, or does this only become urgent for larger companies?
```

### 3. Platform Boundary

```text
When customers onboard into traceability platforms, do they usually already have clean data, or does someone have to clean it first?
```

```text
Is dirty intake data a pre-onboarding blocker for traceability software?
```

```text
Would platforms benefit if someone helped customers clean records before implementation?
```

### 4. Partnership Discovery

Do not start with partnership.

Ask after he understands the pilot:

```text
If this pilot shows that messy intake data is a blocker, could this type of cleanup service be complementary to ENSESO4Food?
```

Then:

```text
What would I need to prove for this to be useful to a traceability platform or consultant?
```

Then:

```text
Would you be open to reviewing the pilot output once I run the first few audits?
```

That is the correct sequence.

## The Best Way To Ask For Help

Do not ask:

**"Can you help me?"**

Ask:

```text
I’m trying to make the pilot useful to the industry rather than just technically interesting.

What would make the output credible to someone like you?
```

This respects his expertise.

## What You Want From The Call

Try to get one of these outcomes:

1. He validates the pilot structure.
2. He tells you the exact fields/documents to check.
3. He tells you which segment to target first.
4. He agrees to review your first pilot output.
5. He introduces one operator, consultant, or traceability person.
6. He says whether this could be complementary to ENSESO4Food.

Do not try to get all six.

One or two is enough.

## Your 60-Second Opening

Use this:

```text
Jim, thanks again for taking the call.

I wanted to give quick context. I’ve been doing field discovery around San Jose with produce wholesalers, restaurant suppliers, and a larger avocado distributor.

The pattern I’m seeing is that smaller and mid-sized operators still receive supplier records as paper invoices, handwritten notes, PDFs, labels, Excel, or systems like DProduce Man. Larger operators have traceability systems, but the harder operational question seems to be what happens when supplier records are missing or conflicting.

So I’m planning a focused Bay Area pilot with a few medium-scale produce or food operators. The first pilot is not a dashboard. It is a cleanup output: 5 redacted shipment record sets in, missing-KDE/mismatch report and clean Excel or FDA-style export out.

I wanted your perspective on whether this pilot is structured correctly and whether this messy pre-platform cleanup layer is a real gap in the market.
```

## If He Says "Our Platform Already Does That"

Say:

```text
That is helpful to know. In that case, I’m trying to understand the boundary.

For customers that are not yet ready for a platform, do you still see a preparation/onboarding step where their paper, Excel, or label records need to be cleaned first?
```

## If He Says "This Is Useful"

Say:

```text
That’s helpful. What would you want to see in the pilot output to know this is real and not just a spreadsheet exercise?
```

Then:

```text
Would you be open to me sending the first sample output for critique?
```

## If He Asks About Product

Say:

```text
The first version is service-led. I want to prove the workflow by delivering the output manually with AI assistance before building a full app.

The software will come from repeated patterns in the pilot: extraction, matching, exception classification, and export.
```

This sounds intentional, not unprepared.

## Final Close

```text
This was very useful.

My next step is to run the pilot with a few local operators and produce a sample audit output.

Would it be okay if I send you the output for critique?

And is there one person or type of company you think I should speak with next?
```

## One-Line Meeting Objective

**Get Jim to help sharpen the pilot, not judge the whole startup.**
