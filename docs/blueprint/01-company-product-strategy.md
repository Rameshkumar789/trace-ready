# Company And Product Strategy

## Company Name

Recommended company name:

> TraceReady

Why it works:

- It is partner-friendly.
- It does not sound like a replacement for traceability platforms.
- It points to readiness, not just compliance paperwork.
- It can expand beyond FSMA 204 later.

## Product Naming

Start with:

> TraceReady Audit

Expansion products:

1. TraceReady Audit  
   Digital FSMA 204 readiness and gap analysis.

2. TraceReady Remediation  
   Managed workflow for supplier gaps, missing KDEs, lot-code issues, and remediation tasks.

3. TraceReady Integrations  
   Export and sync layer into ERP, WMS, EDI, ENSESO4Food, ReposiTrak, Starfish, TagOne, FoodLogiQ, and similar systems.

4. TraceReady Monitor  
   Continuous validation layer that checks incoming supplier data, receiving records, transformation records, outgoing shipment data, EDI/ASN messages, BOLs, invoices, labels, ERP/WMS exports, and traceability plan evidence for FSMA 204 readiness.

Do not pitch these as three equal products. Pitch them as a sequence:

> Audit finds the gap. Remediation fixes the gap. Monitor catches the gap as data flows. Integrations keep clean evidence moving.

## One-Line Description

TraceReady helps food operators discover and fix FSMA 204 traceability gaps before audits, recalls, or platform onboarding.

More precise product description:

> TraceReady is an FSMA 204 validation and exception layer for existing ERP, WMS, EDI, ASN, document, label, and spreadsheet workflows.

## Strong Positioning

Use:

> You may think your current system is traceable. TraceReady shows whether it is actually FSMA 204-ready.

For Jim / platform partners, use:

> TraceReady validates whether a customer's current data flows can produce FSMA 204-ready KDE/TLC/CTE evidence and a sortable FDA-style export before platform onboarding.

Avoid:

> AI software for food traceability.

That is too generic and attracts direct comparison with platforms.

## Market Problem

Many food operators already have "inside-the-walls" traceability:

- inventory comes in,
- inventory moves through a warehouse or facility,
- inventory goes out,
- ERP/WMS may store transactions.

But FSMA 204 readiness requires more:

- covered product identification,
- supplier KDE flow,
- traceability lot code preservation,
- transformation input-output linkage,
- downstream data sharing,
- traceability plan,
- FDA/customer-ready sortable records.

The hidden problem:

> Operators may believe they are compliant because they have inventory traceability, but they may fail FSMA 204 readiness because supplier, lot-code, transformation, and sharing workflows are incomplete.

## First Customer Profile

Best first ICP:

- medium-sized produce distributors,
- packers,
- repackers,
- food hubs,
- fresh-cut processors,
- commissary kitchens,
- operators selling to institutions, schools, retailers, or foodservice buyers.

Avoid as first ICP:

- very tiny operators who do not know or care about FSMA yet,
- large enterprises with long procurement and established teams,
- companies asking for full traceability platform replacement,
- farms only, unless they already have strong buyer pressure.

## Differentiation

TraceReady is not another traceability system of record.

TraceReady is the readiness and remediation layer:

- before platform onboarding,
- before an audit,
- before a recall,
- before a customer requires full traceability exchange,
- around existing ERP/WMS/Excel/DProduce Man workflows.

TraceReady does not run warehouse operations. It validates whether the data produced by warehouse operations is FSMA 204-ready.

TraceReady should answer:

- Is this business/entity in scope?
- Is this product on the Food Traceability List?
- Which CTE happened?
- Which KDEs are required?
- Is the TLC present?
- Was the TLC assigned by the right party or event?
- Was the TLC preserved when no transformation occurred?
- If transformation occurred, was a new TLC assigned and linked to input TLCs?
- Can the required data be shared by EDI/ASN/API/Excel/manual record?
- Can the operator produce a sortable FDA-style export within 24 hours if requested?
- Which supplier, system, document, or workflow is causing the gap?

## Strategic Wedge

The wedge is:

> Digital FSMA 204 readiness audit for medium-sized produce operators.

This is narrow enough to build and sell.

The expansion is:

> AI-native traceability validation, remediation, and exception operations across supplier, receiving, transformation, shipping, EDI/ASN, ERP/WMS, and document flows.

## What Not To Build First

Do not build:

- full receiving/shipping/transform transaction system,
- QR code consumer traceability,
- blockchain provenance,
- complete supplier portal,
- deep ERP integrations,
- fully automated compliance decisioning,
- case-level scanning app,
- customer-specific EDI/ASN engine.

Those are later or partner territory.

Also avoid saying:

- "TraceReady guarantees FSMA compliance,"
- "TraceReady replaces ERP/WMS,"
- "TraceReady is a complete traceability platform,"
- "TraceReady certifies compliance."

Use instead:

- "FSMA 204 readiness,"
- "gap detection,"
- "evidence-backed validation,"
- "human-reviewed audit output,"
- "FDA-style sortable export readiness."

## Moat Over Time

Early moat:

- founder learning,
- expert-reviewed audit template,
- real customer sample data,
- customer trust,
- speed of manual execution.

Medium-term moat:

- product classification rules,
- supplier gap patterns,
- lot-code lineage validation logic,
- remediation playbooks,
- audit benchmarks by segment.

Long-term moat:

- proprietary traceability gap dataset,
- integrations,
- supplier readiness network,
- recurring exception workflow,
- trusted readiness score.
