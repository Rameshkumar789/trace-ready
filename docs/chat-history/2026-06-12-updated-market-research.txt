1. What problem are we solving now?

Jim’s call gave us the clearest problem:

Most food companies think:

“We already have traceability because our ERP tracks what comes in and what goes out.”

But Jim’s point was:

Inside-the-walls traceability is not the same as FSMA 204 readiness.

A company may know inventory internally, but still fail because:

* it does not know which products are on the Food Traceability List,
* it cannot receive required supplier Key Data Elements,
* it changes traceability lot codes when it should not,
* it does not preserve incoming lot-code lineage,
* it does not link source lots to transformed output lots,
* it cannot share required records downstream,
* it has no dashboard showing which suppliers are missing required information.

That is exactly where a digital gap audit fits.

5. What exactly should the first product do?

Product 1: Digital FSMA 204 Gap Audit

The first product should answer three questions.

Question 1: Which products are covered?

Input:

* item master,
* product list,
* supplier list,
* purchase records,
* product descriptions.

Output:

* definitely covered,
* maybe covered,
* not covered,
* needs expert interpretation.

This is valuable because many operators do not know which products are on the Food Traceability List.

⸻

Question 2: Can your system preserve lot-code lineage?

Input:

* receiving records,
* supplier lot codes,
* internal lot codes,
* transformation records,
* outbound shipment records.

Output:

* incoming traceability lot code preserved,
* incoming lot code overwritten without transformation,
* transformation linkage missing,
* source lot not connected to output lot,
* unknown lineage.

This is extremely important because Jim highlighted the rule that companies cannot casually change traceability lot codes unless there is transformation. This becomes one of your most important audit modules.

⸻

Question 3: Can you share required data downstream?

Input:

* outbound shipment records,
* customer records,
* EDI/ASN files,
* export format,
* traceability platform status.

Output:

* customer data-sharing ready,
* missing KDEs,
* missing location data,
* missing supplier data,
* EDI/ASN not ready,
* FDA-style export not ready.

Trace Ready FSMA 204 Gap Report

   Red: 27 products likely on FTL

   Red: incoming lot codes overwritten in 22% of sample records
   Yellow: transformation linkage incomplete in 9 records
   Green: 41 records preserved lot lineage
4. Receiving records
   Red: missing traceability lot code source/reference in 60% of sample
   Yellow: quantities inconsistent in 8 records
   Green: product/quantity present in most records
5. Shipping/customer sharing
   Red: no clear FDA-style export
   Yellow: EDI/ASN has partial data
   Green: customer locations available
6. Remediation checklist
   - classify uncertain products
   - request supplier KDEs from 7 suppliers
   - stop overwriting source traceability lot codes
   - add transformation linkage field
   - create outbound KDE export process

This is much easier to sell than a platform.

4. Foodservice distributors

They receive and ship many items, and may not have perfect supplier KDE flow.

5. Small and mid-sized food operators

Jim emphasized that many small customers do not have unique product IDs, unique location IDs, GS1 discipline, or strong systems.

If it remains manual audits, not venture-scale.

Answer:

Start as audit, productize into automated product classification, lot-code lineage checks, supplier-readiness scoring, transformation-linkage validation, and remediation workflows.

2. Is this a feature inside SGS / ReposiTrak / Trakkey / Starfish?

Could be.

Answer:

We are not replacing platforms. We create the readiness layer before platform adoption and the remediation layer after gaps are found.

3. Does the deadline delay reduce urgency?

Yes. FDA enforcement is now tied to July 2028 timing, reducing pure regulatory panic.  

Answer:

Customer pressure and implementation lead time create urgency. Retailers, wholesalers, and platforms are already moving before enforcement.

4. Will small operators pay?

Some may not.

Answer:

Sell to platforms, auditors, consultants, distributors, food hubs, and groups that need many operators assessed. Use partner/channel model.

5. Can you get data access?

This is a real risk.

Answer:

Start with redacted exports and sample records; no deep integration needed for v1.
